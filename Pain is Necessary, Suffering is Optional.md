Pain is data. It signals damage, misalignment, friction — it is the system working correctly. Suffering is the loop that runs after the [[Signal vs Slop|signal]] has already been received. The distinction is the difference between information and rumination.

**Pain research draws the same line and draws it in four places rather than two.** The biopsychosocial model separates **nociception** — the physiological sensory input — from **pain**, the subjective perception arising from it, from **suffering**, the emotional response, from **pain behaviour**, what a person does while in either [S57]. Two consequences follow for this note. What it calls *pain as data* is nociception: the revised clinical definition states that pain and nociception are different phenomena and that pain cannot be inferred from sensory-neuron activity alone, so by the time it is pain it is already experience rather than signal. And *suffering* is used here more narrowly than the clinical term, which covers any emotional response to an aversive event; this note means one case of it — the loop that continues after the signal has landed. The narrowing is this framework's and the wider category is not being renamed.

**What the distinction gains from being grounded is that it stops being a slogan.** The clinical literature reaches the same conclusion from the other direction: it is suffering, not pain, that brings people to doctors [S57]. One case sits on the boundary and does not classify as suffering — [[The Human Condition|grief]], which is not a loop running past its signal but a system reorganizing around a permanent loss.

[[The Optimization Paradox|Optimization]] requires pain as feedback — without it the system cannot update. Suffering is noise that drowns the signal and prevents the update from landing.

Rumination — the loop that runs past the point of useful signal — is addressed directly in [[AI Psychosis]]. The mechanism is similar: a feedback loop that stops serving its original function and begins degrading the system instead.

[[The Human Algorithm]] is designed to process pain as signal and interrupt suffering as noise — returning the system to a functional state. This is the intent of the framework, not a guaranteed outcome.

[[Constructive Displacement]] extends this same non-escalation logic outward — from how a system processes its own signal to how it responds to harm from outside it.

A system that processes pain honestly and interrupts suffering tends toward [[Peace is the Protocol]] — not as the absence of pain, but as the state where pain serves its function without becoming a permanent operating condition.
