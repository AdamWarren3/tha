Memetic + memory. Transmitted ideas that become stored identity. The point where a meme stops being information you received and becomes a belief you hold.

**The distinction from memetics is not decorative.** Memetics measures a population of ideas moving between hosts; its variables are fidelity, fecundity and longevity — how well a thing copies. The host is substrate. This names something else: one idea, inside one person, at the moment it stops being a claim under consideration and becomes a premise reasoned from. Memetically, an infected host is a carrier and the harm is onward transmission. Here there is nothing to carry: the idea has become part of the apparatus doing the evaluating, which is why it can no longer be evaluated. And memetics has no place for whether a host holds an idea knowingly. That distinction is this concept's whole content.

**The transition is documented, and this note claims no part of discovering it.** In memory research it is called **semanticization**: content drifts from episodic to semantic with time and with repeated exposure, shedding its context while the content itself survives. The finding that matters is stated plainly in that literature — memory traces remain intact but fail to retrieve their associated context [S46]. The governing framework is source monitoring, which lists among its own phenomena cryptomnesia and the incorporation of fiction into fact, and which establishes the mechanism underneath: **source is not stored as a tag attached to a memory but reconstructed at retrieval from perceptual and contextual features**, which is why provenance degrades while content does not [S45].

**There is an instrument.** Tulving's Remember/Know paradigm splits recognition into *I remember* — retrieved with its source — and *I know* — content without spatiotemporal origin. That is the premise-versus-claim distinction, operationalised, with half a century of use behind it.

**Volume is a measured variable, not a rhetorical one.** Items presented in a single context are recollected with their context; **context overload produces decontextualisation and "Know" responses — recognition of the item with no episodic detail at all** [S46]. Delivery rate therefore matters mechanically. A person meeting thirty framings in a day carries thirty unresolved attribution problems, and attribution degrades with load and with time while content does not. Algorithmic delivery supplies framings continuously, unrequested, and supplies them stripped of the features source memory depends on: a named speaker, a setting, an occasion, a reason the thing arrived when it did. That is not an argument that the content is false. It is the observation that provenance is the first thing to go, and that a channel optimised for volume is incidentally optimised against it.

**And it has been measured in that channel.** Participants studying news headlines and tweets remembered social media content better than traditional news content — and the source it carried had no effect on memory performance at all. Advertising source tags were forgotten after a short period, after which attributions were reconstructed by schematic guessing from prior credibility, leading those authors to conclude that labelling untrustworthy sources is of limited use because the label does not survive [S47]. And in the channel that most concerns this framework, the effect has been measured directly: a preregistered study of 184 people who generated ideas both alone and with a model found that a week later, correct attribution had dropped — most steeply where the work was mixed [S56].

**So what this note contributes is a name, not a finding.** The mechanism belongs to memory research and predates every claim made here. What the literature lacks is a word a person can hold — *semanticization* describes what happens to word lists, *source monitoring* names a framework rather than the thing that happens to someone, and neither travels outside a laboratory or connects to belief, identity, or what to do next. **Memnetic is this framework's handle on a documented mechanism.** That is the same contribution [[The Human Algorithm]] claims everywhere else: the mechanism is old, the discernment layer is the work.

[[NLP2|NLP²]] is the recognition that memnetic transmission operates at machine, mind and culture scale simultaneously. [[Epistemic Hygiene]] is the practice of auditing which memnetic infections are running and deciding consciously which to keep — knowing which ideas you chose and which chose you. [[The Human Algorithm]] is the deprogramming and reprogramming protocol. [[Suggestion Without a Session]] describes the delivery conditions under which none of the ordinary marks of provenance are present.

Memnetic transmission is not always passive — conscious adoption of a framework is also memnetic. The distinction is awareness.

## What this note does not claim

**That the term is a discovery, or that the mechanism is unstudied.** Both were checked and both came back occupied. The transition is semanticization [S46]; the mechanism is source monitoring [S45]; the application to feeds and advertising is measured [S47]. **An earlier version of this note offered a falsifier — that if beliefs held as premises turned out no harder to trace than beliefs held as claims, the concept would reduce to a preference over *memetic*. That falsifier was tested and the mechanism survived it.** What did not survive was the novelty claim, and this note is the corrected version.

**That the concept is about AI.** It is not. Ideas become premises in childhood, in family, in religion, in twenty years of the same job, and most of what any person reasons from arrived before any feed existed. The algorithmic channel is one delivery environment and an unusually efficient one; it is not the subject.

**That awareness is protection.** The distinction between chosen and unchosen adoption is real and it is not a defence. Warning people about a manipulation reduces its effect only slightly [S39], and source attribution degrades whether or not a person knows it does.

**Unchecked neighbours, named so the gap stays visible.** Internalisation in developmental psychology, ideology in the critical tradition, and schema in cognitive psychology each cover part of this. The **sleeper effect** — content from a low-credibility source retaining influence after the source is forgotten — is the closest documented relative and may be this mechanism measured directly. None has been checked against this note.
