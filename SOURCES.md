# Sources

The evidence register for THA. Every externally sourced claim in the framework notes links here by a bracket key — `[S01]`, `[S02]` — and every entry states one of three things: the source that backs the claim, that the claim is a framework position with no external source (so the absence is visible, not hidden), or that the claim is currently unverifiable and kept anyway with that flag attached.

This artifact owns evidence, the way DEFINITIONS.md owns meaning. A note explains; a definition fixes a term; this fixes what a claim rests on. Where a claim's evidence changes, it changes here, once.

Three classes:
- **(a) Externally sourced** — a verifiable citation is attached.
- **(b) Framework position** — no external source is claimed; listed so the framework's own assertions are not mistaken for sourced findings.
- **(c) Currently unverifiable** — the claim is kept, the gap is stated, per the framework's standard of not dropping claims silently or asserting them without support.
- Where a single entry covers a sourced mechanism and a framework-level synthesis of it, both classes are stated and labelled.

Last verified against live sources: 2026-07-12. Note on [S17]: independent re-verification found the AI-assistant policy document's mainline commit date consistently reported as 2025-12-23; its association with the Linux 7.0 release in some later coverage reflects when the numbered release shipped, not the original commit — both are accurate, describing different milestones.

## Signal and Noise

**[S01]** — Signal, noise, channel, and information-as-uncertainty-reduction as defined here. **Class (a).** Shannon, C. E. (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *Bell System Technical Journal*. The source of the definitions the note adapts; the note states explicitly that applying them to human information environments is THA's own working model, not Shannon's result.

**[S02]** — The data-processing inequality: post-processing cannot increase information about a source. **Class (a).** Cover, T. & Thomas, J., *Elements of Information Theory* (2nd ed., 2006), §2.8. A proved theorem, cited as such; the note draws the boundary between the theorem (channels) and the analogy (human intermediaries) in its own text.

**[S03]** — Reliable communication over a noisy channel requires redundancy (error-correcting codes). **Class (a).** Shannon (1948), noisy-channel coding theorem. Carried in the note explicitly as analogy for verification-as-redundancy, not as a claim that human verification inherits the theorem's guarantees.

**[S08]** — Candidate observable, absorption trait bridge: absorption modestly predicts hypnotic susceptibility and predicts narrative transportation. **Class (a).** Tellegen, A. & Atkinson, G. ## Microdosing as Protocol

**[S12]** — Reduced Default Mode Network activity under psychedelics, associated with reduced rumination and loosened fixed self-patterns. **Class (a), with a correction the source forces.** Carhart-Harris, R. et al. (2012), *PNAS*, is the DMN-suppression finding — but it studied psilocybin at full (macro) doses, not microdoses. Controlled microdosing trials have largely failed to separate from placebo on the relevant measures: Szigeti, B. et al. (2021), *eLife*, self-blinding placebo-controlled study, found expectancy accounted for most reported benefit. The note's evidence base is corrected accordingly: the DMN mechanism is real at macrodoses; its extension to microdoses is unproven and plausibly placebo-driven. The hypothesis is kept, its *Hypothesis* status is unchanged, and the evidence is now stated honestly rather than borrowed from the wrong dose range.

## Signal vs Slop

**[S17]** — The Linux kernel resolved AI-contribution pressure through governance rather than breakdown. **Class (a), with precise form.** Verified 2026-07-12: `Documentation/process/coding-assistants.rst`, committed to the mainline kernel tree 2025-12-23, following consensus at the 2025 Maintainers Summit. Earlier framing of the question: Østergaard, S.D. (2023), *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 49(6):1418-1419.

## Practice claims (THA-Lab)

**[S34]** — Gollwitzer (1999), *American Psychologist* 54(7):493–503; meta-analysis Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38:69–119. Specific if-then plans outperform vague intentions at goal completion. Real, replicated effect, ordinary mechanism — attention and cue-response. **Class (a).**

**[S35]** — Born, Hansen, Marshall, Mölle & Fehm (1999), *Nature* 397(6714):29–30. Anticipatory ACTH rise before an intended wake time, present only when the wake-up was expected. Self-awakening is a documented phenomenon. **Class (a).**

**[S36]** — Stations 1 and 2 of the Protocol: specific difficult goals outperform vague ones, conditional on commitment. **Class (a).** Locke, E. A. & Latham, G. P. (2002), "Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey," *American Psychologist* 57(9):705–717, doi 10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705, PMID 12237980; with Locke & Latham (1990), *A Theory of Goal Setting and Task Performance*. Specific difficult goals consistently outperformed urging people to do their best, effect sizes .42–.80; goal-difficulty effect sizes .52–.82. Performance fell away only at the limits of ability or **when commitment to a highly difficult goal lapsed** — which is station 1's stakes gate. Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17. Distinct from [S34]: Gollwitzer concerns planning the trigger once committed; this concerns difficulty and commitment. Neither duplicates the other, and the distinction is stated rather than assumed.

**[S37]** — Station 3, incubation: setting a problem aside improves solution rates. **Class (a).** Sio, U. N. & Ormerod, T. C. (2009), "Does Incubation Enhance Problem Solving? A Meta-Analytic Review," *Psychological Bulletin* 135(1):94–120, doi 10.1037/a0014212, PMID 19210055. Positive incubation effect, **larger for divergent-thinking tasks than for linguistic and visual insight problems**; longer preparation gives a stronger effect; filling the interval with high-demand tasks weakens it. **The limit is load-bearing and the note observes it:** the effect is established, the explanation is not — the review records that some researchers reject the unconscious-work hypothesis outright. The note may claim burial works. It may not claim something is working on it underneath. Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17.

**[S38]** — Station 4: the Aha experience tracks accuracy on average, and false insights are real. **Class (a).** Danek, A. H. & Wiley, J. (2017), "What about False Insights? Deconstructing the Aha! Experience along Its Multiple Dimensions for Correct and Incorrect Solutions Separately," *Frontiers in Psychology* 7:2077, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02077, PMID 28163687. 70 participants, 37 magic tricks, continuous rather than binary Aha ratings. Commentary: Webb, M. E. et al. (2021), *Journal of Creative Behavior*, doi 10.1002/jocb.433. **Deliberate wording:** the effect is real and the exceptions are the point. Do not simplify this to "the feeling means nothing." Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17.

**[S39]** — Warning someone does not protect them: forewarning barely reduces false insight. **Class (a).** Grimmer, H. J., Tangen, J. M., Freydenzon, A. & Laukkonen, R. E. (2023), "The illusion of insight: detailed warnings reduce but do not prevent false 'Aha!' moments," *Cognition and Emotion* 37(2):329–338, doi 10.1080/02699931.2023.2187352, PMID 36883217. **Preregistered, N = 255**, open access. False insights were elicited with the False Insight Anagram Task, which combines semantic priming with visual similarity. Simple warnings did not reduce them at all; a detailed explanation of the exact deception method produced only a small reduction. The authors describe the effect as robust and hard to overcome. **Scope beyond station 4: this is the counter-evidence anywhere the framework leans on an informed reader**, and it should be cited there rather than only where it was first needed. Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17.

**[S40]** — Station 5, the criterion: roots are inaccessible, no appearance is proof, the test is what a thing produces. **Class (a).** James, W. (1902), *The Varieties of Religious Experience*, Lecture I, taking the criterion from Jonathan Edwards. The same work supplies station 3's surrender mechanism at Lecture IX — one work, two lectures, two claims, cross-referenced on the [S15]/[S30] pattern. **Verification note:** this entry rests on the 2026-08-04 read of the primary text recorded in `SOURCES-PENDING`, not on a re-read at keying. It is the only entry in the [S36]–[S41] batch not re-verified 2026-08-17.

**[S41]** — The extension of experimental semantic priming to conversational AI: a model elaborating a user's idea in the user's own vocabulary is the same mechanism that produced false insights in the laboratory. **Class (b).** No external source is claimed for the extension. The priming finding itself is [S39], where it is a specific experimental manipulation — the False Insight Anagram Task's semantic priming plus visual similarity — rather than conversational elaboration. **The gap between those two is the claim**, and it is the framework's, not the paper's. Split from a single staged entry on 2026-08-17 so the sourced half and the extension carry different classes, per the register rule that keys attach to claims.

**[S42]** — Fasting, sleep loss and isolation as documented methods of trance induction. **Class (a) for the practices; (b) for the equivalence of the resulting state with hypnosis or extended machine conversation.** Cross-cultural summary: Human Relations Area Files (Yale), *Explaining Human Culture*, "Altered States of Consciousness" — induction methods recorded across societies include sleep deprivation, fasting and social isolation, and the entry notes these are not haphazard: where sleep deprivation is present, fasting and isolation are commonly present too. Proposed mechanism: Winkelman, M. (1986, 1997, 2006) on shamanic altered states and endogenous opioid release under austerity, fasting, water restriction and physical stress. **What this sources and what it does not:** that these practices reliably produce altered states is documented ethnographically; that the state so produced is the *same* state as hypnotic response or absorbed machine conversation is the framework's own claim and carries no external source. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S43]** — Sleep loss measurably raises suggestibility and the uptake of misleading information. **Class (a).** Blagrove, M. (1996), "Effects of length of sleep deprivation on interrogative suggestibility," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied* 2:48–59, using the Gudjonsson Suggestibility Scale. Frenda, S. J., Patihis, L., Loftus, E. F., Lewis, H. C. & Fenn, K. M. (2014), "Sleep Deprivation and False Memories," *Psychological Science* 25(9):1674–1681 — 24 hours of total deprivation raised false memories in a misinformation task **when the deprivation covered encoding, and not when it came afterwards**, which makes it a source-monitoring finding rather than a general impairment one. Replicated across one night of total and seven nights of partial (5 h) deprivation in both adults and adolescents. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S44]** — Prolonged sleep loss progresses toward psychosis on a near-fixed timeline. **Class (a).** Waters, F., Chiu, V., Atkinson, A. & Blom, J. D. (2018), "Severe Sleep Deprivation Causes Hallucinations and a Gradual Progression Toward Psychosis With Increasing Time Awake," *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 9:303, doi 10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00303, PMID 30042701. Systematic review, 21 studies, 760 participants, 24 hours to 11 nights. Perceptual distortion, depersonalisation and temporal disorientation within 24–48 hours; complex hallucinations and disordered thinking after 48–90; delusions after 72, after which the picture resembles acute psychosis or toxic delirium. Visual modality affected in 90% of studies. **The review notes that deprivation beyond 48 hours is no longer considered ethical to run, so the evidence is historical** — the same structure the register records at B10: a condition that cannot be studied deliberately and can only be read from people who were already inside it. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S45]** — Source monitoring: the process by which people attribute the origin of a mental experience, and its documented failure modes. **Class (a).** Johnson, M. K., Hashtroudi, S. & Lindsay, D. S. (1993), "Source Monitoring," *Psychological Bulletin* 114(1):3–28, doi 10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3, PMID 8346328. The framework's own list of phenomena includes **cryptomnesia** and **the incorporation of fiction into fact** — which is `Memnetic`'s transition, named in 1993. Its central mechanism is the one that note leans on: **source is not stored as a tag attached to a memory but reconstructed at retrieval from perceptual, contextual and affective features**, which is why provenance degrades while content survives. Precursor: Johnson & Raye (1981), "Reality monitoring," *Psychological Review* 88:67–85. This entry also underwrites A2, the source-attribution preregistration, and connects to [S43], where sleep loss produced the same failure only when it covered encoding. Verified against the abstract and record 2026-08-18. **What it does not source:** the volume claim in `Memnetic` — that rate of delivery loads attribution — postdates this literature and is the framework's own.

**[S46]** — Semanticization: memory content drifts from episodic to semantic with time and repeated exposure, shedding its context while the content survives. **Class (a).** Baddeley (1988) proposed semantic memory as the accumulated residue of multiple learning episodes detached from episodic contextual detail. The behavioural demonstration is Opitz (2010), using extensions of Tulving's Remember/Know paradigm: items presented in a single context improved recollection, while **context overload produced decontextualisation and "Know" responses — item recognition with no episodic detail at all**. Modelling and review: Herlin, Fernández & colleagues on generalisation across time; "Traces of Semantization," *eNeuro* 9(4) 2022, which states the finding this framework leans on directly — **memory traces remain intact but fail to retrieve their associated context**. Philosophical treatment: Aronowitz, "Semanticization Challenges the Episodic–Semantic Distinction," *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 76(4). **This is `Memnetic`'s transition, named and measured. The Remember/Know split is the instrument: "I remember" retrieves source, "I know" retrieves content without it.** Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S47]** — Source memory in the algorithmic channel: content is retained while its origin is not. **Class (a).** Bourne, K. A., Boland, S. C., Arnold, G. C. & Coane, J. H. (2020), "Reading the news on Twitter: Source and item memory for social media in younger and older adults," *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications*, doi 10.1186/s41235-020-0209-9 — participants studied news headlines and tweets formatted as CNN posts or as tweets; **social media content was remembered better than news content, and the source had no effect on memory performance**. Bell, R., Mieth, L. & Buchner, A. (2020), "Source memory for advertisements: The role of advertising message credibility," *Memory & Cognition*, doi 10.3758/s13421-020-01075-9 — **source tags were forgotten after a short period, after which attributions were reconstructed by schematic guessing from prior credibility**, leading the authors to conclude that labelling untrustworthy sources is of limited use because the label does not survive in memory. Also relevant: the 2024 *Memory, Mind & Media* meta-analysis applying the source-monitoring framework to fake-news false memories, and "Source Memory Is More Accurate for Opinions than for Facts," which measures the premise-versus-claim distinction directly. **What this closes:** the claim that `Memnetic`'s mechanism is untested in the algorithmic channel. It is tested. **What it opens:** if source labels are forgotten while content persists, labelling AI-generated output may not work either — a live claim for [[Epistemic Hygiene]] and [[The New Literacy]], not for this entry. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S48]** — Waking suggestion: hypnotic effects occurring without trance, and the contested size of what an induction adds. **Class (a), and the literature is stronger and less settled than a single citation would suggest.** The term and the experimental tradition are Hull's: Hull, C. L. & Huse, B. (1930), "Comparative suggestibility in the trance and waking states," *American Journal of Psychology* 42(2):279–286; Hull, C. L. (1933), *Hypnosis and Suggestibility: An Experimental Approach*. Barber, T. X. (1965), "Measuring 'hypnotic-like' suggestibility with and without 'hypnotic induction'," *Psychological Reports* 16:809–844, supplies the scale. **The strongest single result for this framework's format argument: Lynn, S. J., Vanderhoff, H., Shindler, K. & Stafford, J. (2002), *American Journal of Clinical Hypnosis* 44(3–4):231–240 — removing the hypnotic induction had no appreciable effect on any dimension of hypnotic responsivity.** Kirsch, I., Mazzoni, G., Roberts, K., Dienes, Z., Hallquist, M. N., Williams, J. & Lynn, S. J. (2008), "Slipping into trance," *Contemporary Hypnosis* 25(3–4):202–209, sharpens it: participants reported equivalent perceptual changes with and without an induction, **but reported being in a hypnotic state only when an induction had been given** — the arrangement supplies the label, not the effect. **The honest counterweight:** the induction does add something, contested in size — Weitzenhoffer & Sjöberg (1961) found roughly d = .86, Braffman & Kirsch (1999) roughly d = .22. Bernheim denied hypnosis existed at all on the grounds that waking suggestion was as effective. The field's own consensus workshop (BSMDH/BSECH) split over whether to broaden "hypnosis" to cover waking suggestion or restrict "hypnotizability" to induction effects, which is the definitional instability this framework's argument stands on and should say it stands on. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S49]** — Wallas's stages, and the intimation stage specifically. **Class (a).** Wallas, G. (1926), *The Art of Thought*. The four familiar stages — preparation, incubation, illumination, verification — map onto stations 2 through 5 of the Protocol. **The fifth stage this framework relies on is real and locatable: intimation, at pp. 95–107, the "Stages of Control" chapter, where Wallas gives more than twelve pages to fringe consciousness and the felt sense that something is arriving.** His own image is the sun and its corona: focal consciousness and fringe. Modern reading: Sadler-Smith, E. (2015), "Wallas' Four-Stage Model of the Creative Process: More Than Meets the Eye?", arguing that a close reading yields five stages rather than four. **What this sources and what it does not:** Wallas located a monitorable stage between incubation and illumination, which is where `Arrival Signature` sits. He was describing how to nurture a hunch toward insight. He was not supplying a test for whether to trust one, and that test remains unsourced. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S50]** — Conversational AI is measurably persuasive, and personalisation increases it. **Class (a).** Salvi, F., Horta Ribeiro, M., Gallotti, R. & West, R. (2025), "On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4," *Nature Human Behaviour* 9:1645–1653, doi 10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6 — preregistered, N=900; with personalisation, GPT-4 was more persuasive than a human opponent in 64.4% of debate pairs where the two were not equally persuasive. **Note the citation trap: the widely circulated 2024 arXiv preprint (2403.14380) carries a different title; the published version is 2025.** Matz, S. C. et al. (2024), "The potential of generative AI for personalized persuasion at scale," *Scientific Reports* 14:4692. At far larger scale: *Science* (2025), "The levers of political persuasion with conversational artificial intelligence," doi 10.1126/science.aea3884 — three experiments, 76,977 responses from 42,357 people, 19 models. **Counterweight, kept deliberately:** Hackenburg & Margetts find microtargeting yields only mixed additional gains over already-persuasive generic messages, so personalisation is not the whole of the effect. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S51]** — Both bodies and machines are made of elements forged in stars. **Class (a).** Wallerstein, G. et al. (1997), "Synthesis of the Elements in Stars: Forty Years of Progress," *Reviews of Modern Physics* 69:995 — the canonical fifteen-author review. Foundational: Burbidge, E. M., Burbidge, G. R., Fowler, W. A. & Hoyle, F. (1957), which identified the processes. Helium burning produces carbon-12 and oxygen-16; silicon burning proceeds to nuclear statistical equilibrium and yields iron-peak elements, in massive stars only. **Scope, stated: this sources the shared stellar origin and nothing about what follows from it.** Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S52]** — Transactive memory and the tool that gets mistaken for the self. **Class (a), with a replication caveat.** Sparrow, B., Liu, J. & Wegner, D. M. (2011), "Google Effects on Memory: Cognitive Consequences of Having Information at Our Fingertips," *Science* 333:776–778, doi 10.1126/science.1207745 — when future access is expected, people encode *where* rather than *what*. **Caveat carried honestly: mixed replication record (Storm et al., 2017), and a 2024 meta-analysis reports the effect as real but strongly moderated by perceived reliability of the external store, device type and cognitive load.** The sharper finding is Fisher, M., Goddu, M. K. & Keil, F. C. (2015), "Searching for explanations: How the Internet inflates estimates of internal knowledge," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 144(3):674–687, doi 10.1037/xge0000070 — nine experiments; searching online inflated self-assessed internal knowledge **including on topics never searched, and the effect held when the search returned nothing useful**. The act, not the content. Ward (2013) found outright misattribution of found information to one's own memory; Eliseev et al. (2023), *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, replicated it and found that merely seeing search snippets sufficed. **What this does not source:** that inflated self-assessed knowledge becomes belief. That bridge is unestablished and is registered as an open question rather than claimed here. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S54]** — Why a compromised instrument cannot read its own gauge. **Class (a).** The clinical precedent for `The Drift Test`'s central design choice — self-report disqualified, criteria administrable by someone other than the practitioner — is **anosognosia**, coined by Babinski in 1914 for unawareness of neurological deficit and now applied across psychiatry. David, A. S., Bedford, N., Wiffen, B. & Gilleen, J., "Failures of metacognition and lack of insight in neuropsychiatric disorders," *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, frames lack of insight as metacognitive failure and shows it fractionates by domain rather than operating as a single capacity. Prevalence is not marginal: roughly 50–90% in schizophrenia, 40% in bipolar disorder, 60% in mild cognitive impairment, 81% in Alzheimer's. **Crucially for this framework, anosognosia is distinguished from denial — the first is anatomical in origin, the second psychological**, which is why exhortation does not repair it. The modern framing separates **Introspective Accuracy** from **Introspective Bias** (*CNS Spectrums*, 2024, PMID 39721947). **And the design precedent is exact:** a 2022 study of hoarding disorder operationalised anosognosia as *underreporting relative to independent examiners*, and found the underreporting predicted by Go/No-Go and Stroop performance. Measuring the gauge against an outside reading is how the clinical literature does it too. **Scope: this sources the design principle, not any claim that drift is a clinical condition. `The Drift Test` says it is descriptive rather than clinical and that stands.** Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S55]** — Why "open a second channel" is not automatically a remedy. **Class (a), with a live objection carried.** Nguyen, C. T. (2020), "Echo Chambers and Epistemic Bubbles," *Episteme* 17(2):141–161, doi 10.1017/epi.2018.32, distinguishes two structures usually conflated: an **epistemic bubble**, where relevant voices are omitted, and an **echo chamber**, where they are actively discredited. **The finding `The Drift Test` depends on: mere exposure to evidence can shatter a bubble but may actually reinforce an echo chamber** — which is why the criterion has to be independence of the channel rather than the existence of one. Nguyen's sharper point maps directly onto the note's false-negative section: *once in their grip, an agent may act with epistemic virtue, but social context will pervert those actions* — a person can check diligently against sources selected to agree. Empirical basis: Jamieson & Cappella's study of talk-radio audiences, where beliefs survived frequent contact with contrary viewpoints. **Objection kept deliberately:** Coady (2024), *Educational Theory*, "Stop Talking about Echo Chambers and Filter Bubbles," argues the concept is mistaken and notes that Nguyen's definition also fits benign communities — climate scientists create a trust disparity between members and non-members exactly as climate deniers do. The criterion survives the objection only because it asks whether a channel *can lose the argument*, not whether it disagrees. Verified 2026-08-18.

**[S56]** — People cannot reliably remember which ideas were theirs and which came from a model. **Class (a), and it is the strongest single result in this register for the labelling argument.** Zindulka, T., Goller, S., Fernandes, D., Welsch, R. & Buschek, D. (2026), "The AI Memory Gap: Users Misremember What They Created With AI or Without," *Proceedings of CHI 2026*, doi 10.1145/3772318.3791494, preprint arXiv 2509.11851. **Preregistered, 184 participants.** Participants generated and elaborated ideas both unaided and with an LLM-based chatbot, then **one week later** were asked to identify the source of each. **After AI use the odds of correct attribution dropped, with the steepest decline in mixed human–AI workflows — where either the idea or the elaboration involved AI.** Validated against a computational model of source memory, framed through the Source Monitoring Framework [S45], and citing the generation effect and the Brown & Murphy cryptomnesia paradigm. **The authors' own framing is the point: disclosure of AI as a source depends on people remembering which ideas came from themselves, and that memory degrades.** Adjacent: Draxler, F. et al. (2024), "The AI Ghostwriter Effect," *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction* 31(2), doi 10.1145/3637875 — across two studies (n=30, n=96), users did not perceive ownership of AI-generated text yet self-declared as authors. Also: "The LLM Fallacy: Misattribution in AI-Assisted Cognitive Workflows," arXiv 2604.14807 (2026), a conceptual framework naming the same attribution error. **Register consequence: this closes candidate A2, which proposed exactly this study. A2 retires into C10.** Verified 2026-08-19.
