# Sources

The evidence register for THA. Every externally sourced claim in the framework notes links here by a bracket key — `[S01]`, `[S02]` — and every entry states one of three things: the source that backs the claim, that the claim is a framework position with no external source (so the absence is visible, not hidden), or that the claim is currently unverifiable and kept anyway with that flag attached.

This artifact owns evidence, the way DEFINITIONS.md owns meaning. A note explains; a definition fixes a term; this fixes what a claim rests on. Where a claim's evidence changes, it changes here, once.

Three classes:
- **(a) Externally sourced** — a verifiable citation is attached.
- **(b) Framework position** — no external source is claimed; listed so the framework's own assertions are not mistaken for sourced findings.
- **(c) Currently unverifiable** — the claim is kept, the gap is stated, per the framework's standard of not dropping claims silently or asserting them without support.
- Where a single entry covers a sourced mechanism and a framework-level synthesis of it, both classes are stated and labelled.

Last verified against live sources: 2026-07-12. Note on [S17]: independent re-verification found the AI-assistant policy document's mainline commit date consistently reported as 2025-12-23; its association with the Linux 7.0 release in some later coverage reflects when the numbered release shipped, not the original commit — both are accurate, describing different milestones.

## Signal and Noise

**[S01]** — Signal, noise, channel, and information-as-uncertainty-reduction as defined here. **Class (a).** Shannon, C. E. (1948), "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *Bell System Technical Journal*. The source of the definitions the note adapts; the note states explicitly that applying them to human information environments is THA's own working model, not Shannon's result.

**[S02]** — The data-processing inequality: post-processing cannot increase information about a source. **Class (a).** Cover, T. & Thomas, J., *Elements of Information Theory* (2nd ed., 2006), §2.8. A proved theorem, cited as such; the note draws the boundary between the theorem (channels) and the analogy (human intermediaries) in its own text.

**[S03]** — Reliable communication over a noisy channel requires redundancy (error-correcting codes). **Class (a).** Shannon (1948), noisy-channel coding theorem. Carried in the note explicitly as analogy for verification-as-redundancy, not as a claim that human verification inherits the theorem's guarantees.

## NLP²

**[S04]** — NLP's specific claimed techniques (representational systems, eye-accessing cues, submodalities) have not held up under testing. **Class (a).** Witkowski, T. (2010), "Thirty-Five Years of Research on Neuro-Linguistic Programming," *Polish Psychological Bulletin*; Sturt, J. et al. (2012), systematic review, *British Journal of General Practice*. Both find no reliable empirical support for the core techniques.

**[S05]** — The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis has partial empirical support. **Class (a).** Winawer, J. et al. (2007), *PNAS*, on Russian blue-shade discrimination; Boroditsky, L. (2001), *Cognitive Psychology*, on time metaphor. Support for a weak (influence) reading; the strong (determinism) reading is not supported — the note's "partial" is accurate.

**[S06]** — Hypnosis produces documented neurological changes, not merely behavioural compliance. **Class (a), with a limit the imaging literature states about itself.** Jiang, H., White, M. P., Greicius, M. D., Waelde, L. C. & Spiegel, D. (2017), "Brain Activity and Functional Connectivity Associated with Hypnosis," *Cerebral Cortex* 27(8):4083–4093, doi [10.1093/cercor/bhw220](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhw220), PMID [27469596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27469596/) — 57 subjects selected from 545 on two hypnotisability scales; reduced dorsal anterior cingulate activity, increased executive-control-to-insula connectivity, reduced executive-control-to-default-mode connectivity.

**Corroborated 2026-08-20, because a claim this structural should not rest on one imaging study — and neuroimaging in particular should not.** Landry, M., Lifshitz, M. & Raz, A. (2017), "Brain correlates of hypnosis: A systematic review and meta-analytic exploration," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 81(A):75–98, doi [10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.02.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2017.02.020) — 15 neuroimaging studies pooled; the most consistent finding is activation of the **lingual gyrus**, a higher-order visual region, which the authors read as evidence for the centrality of mental imagery. Wolf, T. G., Faerber, K. A., Rummel, C., Halsband, U. & Campus, G. (2022), "Functional Changes in Brain Activity Using Hypnosis: A Systematic Review," *Brain Sciences* 12(1):108, doi [10.3390/brainsci12010108](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12010108) — PROSPERO-registered, across fMRI, PET, SPECT and EEG. Converging network findings: Deeley, Q. et al. (2012), "Modulating the default mode network using hypnosis," *International Journal of Clinical and Experimental Hypnosis*, PMID [22443526](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22443526/) — attentional absorption after induction associated with reduced default-mode activity and increased prefrontal attentional activity under invariant stimulation; Demertzi, A. et al. (2011), *Progress in Brain Research*, PMID [21854971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21854971/) — reduced lateral frontoparietal connectivity relative to mental imagery.

**The limit, stated by the literature rather than by this framework: *few reliable brain patterns emerge across studies* (Landry et al.).** Three networks recur — executive control, salience, default mode — but the specific regions vary study to study, and a 2023 fMRI study of 50 hypnosis-experienced participants found the neural correlates of two hypnotic states differed only modestly from their controls (de Matos, N. M. P. et al., *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 17:1286336, doi [10.3389/fnhum.2023.1286336](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2023.1286336)). **What this entry supports is that hypnosis has neural correlates distinguishable from ordinary waking attention. It does not support a specific neural signature, and no note should claim one.**

**[S07]** — "The medium is the message"; media formats program perception independently of content. **Class (a).** McLuhan, M. (1964), *Understanding Media*. Primary source for the attributed claim.

**[S08]** — Candidate observable, absorption trait bridge: absorption modestly predicts hypnotic susceptibility and predicts narrative transportation. **Class (a).** Tellegen, A. & Atkinson, G. (1974), *Journal of Abnormal Psychology* (absorption/hypnotizability); Green, M. & Brock, T. (2000), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* (transportation); Lifshitz, M., van Elk, M. & Luhrmann, T. M. (2019), "Absorption and spiritual experience: A review of evidence and potential mechanisms," *Consciousness and Cognition* 73:102760, doi [10.1016/j.concog.2019.05.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2019.05.008), PMID [31228696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31228696/), extending the trait to spiritual experience — high scorers on the Tellegen scale report vivid experiences of hearing God's voice during prayer, and intense responses to psychedelics, placebo brain stimulation, natural beauty, virtual reality and music (verified 2026-08-17). Supports the trait bridge the note offers as a *beginning* of a test, not a completed one. The illusory-truth effect — Hasher, Goldstein & Toppino (1977), *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* — supports the repetition marker specifically. Note: the TV-alpha-wave EEG claim (Krugman 1971) is deliberately excluded — single unreplicated study, insufficient to carry the marker.

## Microdosing as Protocol

**[S12]** — Reduced Default Mode Network activity under psychedelics, associated with reduced rumination and loosened fixed self-patterns. **Class (a), with a correction the source forces.** Carhart-Harris, R. et al. (2012), *PNAS*, is the DMN-suppression finding — but it studied psilocybin at full (macro) doses, not microdoses. Controlled microdosing trials have largely failed to separate from placebo on the relevant measures: Szigeti, B. et al. (2021), *eLife*, self-blinding placebo-controlled study, found expectancy accounted for most reported benefit. The note's evidence base is corrected accordingly: the DMN mechanism is real at macrodoses; its extension to microdoses is unproven and plausibly placebo-driven. The hypothesis is kept, its *Hypothesis* status is unchanged, and the evidence is now stated honestly rather than borrowed from the wrong dose range.

## Signal vs Slop

**[S16]** — The curl AI-slop bug-bounty case. **Class (a).** Verified 2026-07-12, independently reconfirmed: Stenberg ended curl's HackerOne bounty at the end of January 2026 (last accepted submission January 31) after a flood of AI-generated reports that produced no valid vulnerabilities — his own announcement, [The end of the curl bug-bounty](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/01/26/the-end-of-the-curl-bug-bounty/), records 87 confirmed vulnerabilities and over US$100,000 paid across the program's six years, with the confirmed-vulnerability rate falling from above 15% to below 5% through 2025. He named the pattern in [Death by a thousand slops](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2025/07/14/death-by-a-thousand-slops/). **What happened next is not a reopening and the distinction matters.** In [curl security moves again](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/02/25/curl-security-moves-again/) (25 February 2026) Stenberg states it plainly: *the reward money is still gone, there is no bug-bounty, no money for vulnerability reports* — what returned to HackerOne on 1 March 2026 was unpaid vulnerability reporting, reversing a simultaneous move to GitHub he calls *a mistake*. The bounty has not been reinstated. **And the causal direction runs the other way from the obvious reading:** *since we dropped the bounty, the inflow tsunami has dried out substantially.* The flood receded when the money stopped, not when the reports improved. That is a claim about incentive rather than about AI, and it is the version the corpus should carry. Verified against the primary post 2026-08-20.

**[S17]** — The Linux kernel resolved AI-contribution pressure through governance rather than breakdown. **Class (a), with precise form.** Verified 2026-07-12: `Documentation/process/coding-assistants.rst`, committed to the mainline kernel tree 2025-12-23, following consensus at the 2025 Maintainers Summit. The mechanism is an `Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION` commit trailer, plus a hard rule that AI agents may not add `Signed-off-by` tags (only a human can certify the Developer Certificate of Origin). The `Assisted-by` disclosure is recommended, not enforced — Sasha Levin, the policy's author, has stated enforcement is deliberately avoided. The note's "ordinary governance, not a breakdown" reading is accurate; this entry pins the exact form.

## AI Psychosis

**[S18]** — AI hallucination as confident, plausible, unverified model output; engagement-optimized models deepening uncritical-use loops. **Class (a) for the hallucination mechanism; (a) for the "AI psychosis" construct as a named, peer-reviewed framework, with the limit noted below; (b) for the framework's synthesis of the two into a single loop.** Ji, Z. et al. (2023), *ACM Computing Surveys*, "Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation," documents the hallucination mechanism. The construct is no longer unsourced: Hudon, A. & Stip, E., "Delusional Experiences Emerging From AI Chatbot Interactions or 'AI Psychosis'," *JMIR Mental Health* 2025;12:e85799, doi [10.2196/85799](https://doi.org/10.2196/85799), PMID [41273266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41273266/), published 3 December 2025, peer-reviewed and open access. It draws on phenomenological psychopathology, the stress-vulnerability model, cognitive theory and digital mental health research, and uses the term as a descriptive and heuristic label rather than a proposed diagnostic entity — the position AI Psychosis.md already holds. Descriptive and sourced are not in tension; the previous wording implied they were. **Limit:** it is a Viewpoint rather than an empirical study, and it states that it makes no causal claims and offers its pathways as heuristic pending empirical evaluation. **Nothing in this entry supports a causal claim, and the framework does not make one.** Earlier framing of the question: Østergaard, S.D. (2023), *Schizophrenia Bulletin* 49(6):1418-1419, doi [10.1093/schbul/sbad128](https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sbad128).

**The empirical anchor, added 2026-08-20.** Buck, B. & Maheux, A. J. (2026), "Psychosis Risk and Generative Artificial Intelligence Use Frequency, Motivations, and Delusion-Like Experiences: Cross-Sectional Survey Study," *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 28:e85038, doi [10.2196/85038](https://doi.org/10.2196/85038), PMID [41785452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41785452/) — N=1,003 US young adults. **People at elevated psychosis risk were no more likely to have ever used generative AI, but were significantly more likely to use it intensively (odds ratios 1.70–2.56), to seek emotional support from it, and to engage it on pre-existing aberrant beliefs including paranoia, grandiosity and conspiracy thinking.** This is the first empirical counterpart to a literature that had been almost entirely case reports and commentary. **It is cross-sectional, so it establishes association and not direction** — whether intensive use amplifies the beliefs or the beliefs drive intensive use is exactly what it cannot say, and the framework should not imply otherwise.

## The Collective Layer

**[S26]** — Moods and behaviors spread between people in direct contact (a smile prompting a smile, kindness prompting kindness); larger multi-remove network claims are contested. **Class (a), matching the note's own hedge.** Direct emotional-contagion evidence: Hatfield, E., Cacioppo, J. & Rapson, R. (1994), *Emotional Contagion*, Cambridge University Press. **Corroborated 2026-08-20: the phenomenon has since been demonstrated experimentally at scale and reviewed meta-analytically.** Kramer, A. D. I., Guillory, J. E. & Hancock, J. T. (2014), "Experimental evidence of massive-scale emotional contagion through social networks," *PNAS* 111(24):8788–8790, doi [10.1073/pnas.1320040111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320040111) — an experimental manipulation of 689,003 people's news feeds shifted the emotional valence of their own subsequent posts, establishing that contagion operates through text alone, without face-to-face contact or nonverbal cues. **That study is also the standard case in research ethics for experimentation without consent**, which is a second reason this framework should cite it. Herrando, C. & Constantinides, E. (2021), "Emotional Contagion: A Brief Overview and Future Directions," *Frontiers in Psychology* 12:712606, doi [10.3389/fpsyg.2021.712606](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.712606) — review covering the extension from physical to digital settings. ## Practice claims (THA-Lab)

**[S34]** — Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999), "Implementation Intentions: Strong Effects of Simple Plans," *American Psychologist* 54(7):493–503, doi [10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493); meta-analysis Gollwitzer, P. M. & Sheeran, P. (2006), "Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-Analysis of Effects and Processes," *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38:69–119, doi [10.1016/S0065-2601(06](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06))38002-1. Specific if-then plans outperform vague intentions at goal completion. Real, replicated effect, ordinary mechanism — attention and cue-response. **Class (a).**

**[S35]** — Born, J., Hansen, K., Marshall, L., Mölle, M. & Fehm, H. L. (1999), "Timing the end of nocturnal sleep," *Nature* 397(6714):29–30, doi [10.1038/16166](https://doi.org/10.1038/16166). **Single study, small sample, and the register says so.** The finding — an anticipatory adrenocorticotropin rise in the hour before an intended waking time, present only when the wake-up was expected — has been replicated in the self-awakening literature but not at a scale that would make it robust on its own. **It is carried here as suggestive rather than established**, and no note should lean on it for more than the observation that intention can influence a physiological process during sleep. Anticipatory ACTH rise before an intended wake time, present only when the wake-up was expected. Self-awakening is a documented phenomenon. **Class (a).**

**[S36]** — Stations 1 and 2 of the Protocol: specific difficult goals outperform vague ones, conditional on commitment. **Class (a).** Locke, E. A. & Latham, G. P. (2002), "Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey," *American Psychologist* 57(9):705–717, doi [10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705), PMID [12237980](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12237980/); with Locke & Latham (1990), *A Theory of Goal Setting and Task Performance*. Specific difficult goals consistently outperformed urging people to do their best, effect sizes .42–.80; goal-difficulty effect sizes .52–.82. Performance fell away only at the limits of ability or **when commitment to a highly difficult goal lapsed** — which is station 1's stakes gate. Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17. Distinct from [S34]: Gollwitzer concerns planning the trigger once committed; this concerns difficulty and commitment. Neither duplicates the other, and the distinction is stated rather than assumed.

**[S37]** — Station 3, incubation: setting a problem aside improves solution rates. **Class (a).** Sio, U. N. & Ormerod, T. C. (2009), "Does Incubation Enhance Problem Solving? A Meta-Analytic Review," *Psychological Bulletin* 135(1):94–120, doi [10.1037/a0014212](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014212), PMID [19210055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19210055/). Positive incubation effect, **larger for divergent-thinking tasks than for linguistic and visual insight problems**; longer preparation gives a stronger effect; filling the interval with high-demand tasks weakens it. **The limit is load-bearing and the note observes it:** the effect is established, the explanation is not — the review records that some researchers reject the unconscious-work hypothesis outright. The note may claim burial works. It may not claim something is working on it underneath. Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17.

**[S38]** — Station 4: the Aha experience tracks accuracy on average, and false insights are real. **Class (a).** Danek, A. H. & Wiley, J. (2017), "What about False Insights? Deconstructing the Aha! Experience along Its Multiple Dimensions for Correct and Incorrect Solutions Separately," *Frontiers in Psychology* 7:2077, doi [10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02077](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02077), PMID [28163687](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28163687/). 70 participants, 37 magic tricks, continuous rather than binary Aha ratings. Commentary: Webb, M. E. et al. (2021), *Journal of Creative Behavior*, doi [10.1002/jocb.433](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.433). **Deliberate wording:** the effect is real and the exceptions are the point. Do not simplify this to "the feeling means nothing." Verified against the primary text 2026-08-17.

**[S39]** — Warning someone does not protect them: forewarning barely reduces false insight. **Class (a).** Grimmer, H. J., Tangen, J. M., Freydenzon, A. & Laukkonen, R. E. (2023), "The illusion of insight: detailed warnings reduce but do not prevent false 'Aha!' moments," *Cognition and Emotion* 37(2):329–338, doi [10.1080/02699931.2023.2187352](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2023.2187352), PMID [36883217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36883217/). **Preregistered, N = 255**, open access. False insights were elicited with the False Insight Anagram Task, which combines semantic priming with visual similarity. Simple warnings did not reduce them at all; a detailed explanation of the exact deception method produced only a small reduction. The authors describe the effect as robust and hard to overcome. **Scope beyond station 4: this is the counter-evidence anywhere the framework leans on an informed reader**, and it should be cited there rather than only where it was first needed.

**Corroboration added 2026-08-20, because a claim keyed in three notes should not rest on one study.** Three independent lines converge on it from a different literature — the continued-influence effect rather than false insight.

**Same finding, different paradigm, thirteen years earlier.** Ecker, U. K. H., Lewandowsky, S. & Tang, D. T. W. (2010), "Explicit warnings reduce but do not eliminate the continued influence of misinformation," *Memory & Cognition* 38(8):1087–1100, doi [10.3758/MC.38.8.1087](https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.38.8.1087) — a *specific* warning describing the effect in detail reduced continued reliance without eliminating it; a *general* warning was less effective still.

**Quantified against the alternatives.** Walter, N. & Tukachinsky, R. **[S54]** — Why a compromised instrument cannot read its own gauge. **Class (a).** The clinical precedent for `The Drift Test`'s central design choice — self-report disqualified, criteria administrable by someone other than the practitioner — is **anosognosia**, coined by Babinski in 1914 for unawareness of neurological deficit and now applied across psychiatry. (2014), "Anosognosia in Schizophrenia: Hidden in Plain Sight," *Innovations in Clinical Neuroscience* 11(5–6):10–17), and the frequently quoted **81% for Alzheimer's is the top of a 20–81% range** reported by Sevush, S. & Leve, N. (1993), *American Journal of Psychiatry* 150(5):787–791, not a typical value. Figures for mild cognitive impairment span roughly 20–80% across studies. (2022), "Anosognosia in hoarding disorder is predicted by alterations in cognitive and inhibitory control," *Scientific Reports* 12(1):21103, doi [10.1038/s41598-022-25532-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-25532-4), PMID [36526652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36526652/), operationalised anosognosia as *underreporting of clutter relative to independent examiners*, and found the underreporting predicted by Go/No-Go and Stroop performance. **[S55]** — Why "open a second channel" is not automatically a remedy. **Class (a), with a live objection carried.** Nguyen, C. T. **[S57]** — Nociception, pain, suffering and pain behaviour as four distinct levels. **Class (a), and it grounds a distinction this framework had been asserting.** Engel, G. L.
